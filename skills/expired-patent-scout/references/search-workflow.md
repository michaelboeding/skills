# Search workflow

Read after the target area is established. Use current primary evidence for ownership, patent content, and status; broad search tools help locate that evidence.

## Resolve the target

Build a small entity map: user-facing brand/product, company names over time, subsidiaries, documented acquisitions, assignee spellings, inventor leads, and source URLs. Search historical names as well as current owners. Product manuals, official virtual-patent-marking pages, and recorded assignments can connect a product or portfolio; mark the date and strength of each connection. A current brand owner may not own every historical patent, and a recorded assignment is not a complete legal title opinion.

For a category without brands, translate the user need into components/functions and synonyms. Derive relevant CPC/IPC classes from genuinely relevant seed patents, then search neighboring mechanisms. Utility inventions and design/registered-design rights need distinct analysis; confirm which matter to the user's goal.

## Search in layers

1. Search assignee names and aliases with the product/function terms. Also search the product's official patent markings and manuals.
2. Search the technology independently of owner using synonyms, classifications, and relevant features. Do not assume useful ideas must come from the named competitors.
3. Follow backward citations for underlying mechanisms and forward citations/family links for later developments. Read the claims before treating a citation as substantive overlap.
4. Deduplicate publications of the same grant/family. Retain each grant/application number, kind code, territory, and related member's individual status.
5. Screen possible recent expirations using term/lapse facts, then obtain the records needed for verification. A filing-date search is only candidate generation, never the final recency filter.

Use search syntax supported by the actual service; verify filters rather than copying an untested universal query. Log the exact queries, classifications, coverage bounds, source update information, records screened, exclusions, and access failures. If the scope is large, perform broad triage and explain the subset selected for deep review. Return fewer strong leads instead of implying an exhaustive search from a handful of results.

## Source roles

| Need | Sources and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Broad discovery and family leads | Patent search databases, official search portals, citations, and public patent publications; aggregated status is a lead |
| Patent mechanism/claims/figures | The actual published/granted patent document and relevant post-grant changes, with page/claim/figure locators |
| U.S. file and status facts | Patent Center, maintenance-fee records/notices, official term adjustment/extension/disclaimer records, and later reinstatement events |
| Brand/portfolio association | Company materials, product patent markings, official assignment records, and documented historical ownership |
| European post-grant status | European Patent Register plus the relevant national register or Unitary Patent record, as appropriate |
| Other markets | The responsible national/regional office and local rules; do not export U.S. assumptions |

Use authenticated browser access only when available and authorized. Read its tool instructions before use. If a record needs a sign-in, offer the user a narrow access step or an official exported record; continue independent research and mark the blocked verification. Do not bypass access restrictions, claim a record was inspected when it was not, or treat a failed fetch as proof of expiration.

Save source IDs, URLs, title/office, access date, event/document date, and exact record/document locators. Save public extracts or screenshots when a portal link is session-bound, preserving enough information to retrieve the same file. A timestamped official download can support historical facts; a current-status statement needs a fresh status check with latency limits disclosed.

## Claims-to-product analysis

For each leading opportunity, map the proposed product feature to specific granted claims and the useful teaching in the specification. Distinguish observed patent content, verified product linkage, and the agent's product concept. A broad claim summary is not a formal infringement opinion.

Discuss implementation dependencies the disclosure may not solve: materials, tolerances, manufacturing process, firmware, calibration, suppliers, or tested performance. Prefer a concrete prototype question over vague "copy this product" advice. Reuse opportunities can involve a mechanism or subassembly even if other aspects of a modern product need separate clearance.

Rank by target fit, practical usefulness, feasibility assumptions, possible differentiation, status evidence, and remaining review effort. Do not hide an active related claim behind a high commercial score. Provide an initial potential-rights map with patent IDs, claim-level concern, country, status source, and unresolved questions.

## Primary entry points

- [USPTO patent search](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/search)
- [USPTO Patent Center](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/apply/patent-center)
- [USPTO ownership and assignment search](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/maintain/patents-assignments-change-search-ownership)
- [USPTO maintenance information](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/maintain)
- [European Patent Register](https://www.epo.org/en/searching-for-patents/legal/register)

These are entry points, not evidence that any particular patent has expired. Open the actual candidate record and cite it in the result.
